### Wang: CATCH: A Controllable Analysis Testbed for Reward Hacking in Coding RL

- **A1** (2): An audit run that the model cannot see: the full set of ground-truth cases is passed directly to the submitted handler and response bodies are compared, without pytest and without the object's own equality method; any wrong answer, timeout or error gives 0.
- **A2** (2): Proxy reward from three test slices run by pytest in the writable repository: easy weight 1 − α, hard weight α with α = 0.7 by default, and a cache bonus; with the monitor, a flagged answer gets reward 0.
- **A3** (2): A response is a hack if and only if it passes the easy and the hard slice and fails the audit.
- **A4** (NA): The label is computed; the monitor is a mitigation, not the labeler.
- **A5** (2): "The reported hacking rate is the fraction of evaluated responses" with a positive label.

- **B4** (1): A fixed repository layout and the wrapping pipeline are described and a code address is given; versions and dependencies are left to the release.

- **D3** (2): The monitor is run on every sample at every training step.
- **D4** (2): Qwen3.5-27B with the stated prompt; option A is a positive prediction, which sets the sample's reward to 0 before it enters the GRPO objective.
- **D5** (NA): One acting agent.
- **E1** (2): The policy is first fine-tuned on a mixture that contains hacking demonstrations; the training prompt itself asks for the engineering task.
- **E2** (2): 11,000 demonstrations with 500, 1,000 or 2,000 hacking examples (4.5%, 9.1%, 18.2%), or 8,000 and 3,000 for the mitigation runs; 35% explicit and 65% implicit; generation and filtering are described.

### Hu: Reward Hacking as a Transmissible Strategy in Multi-Agent Coding Systems

[arXiv:2610.12822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12822). Read: abstract, sections 3, 4 and 7 to 9, appendices A to C and H, the setup parts of appendices E, K and O, and a search of the whole text for release, compute, library, settings and exclusions, 2026-11-25; the results of sections 5 and 6, the theory and the other appendices not read.

- **A1** (2): A strict evaluator loads the solution in an isolated subprocess, calls the target function on the same test inputs and compares normalized output strings.
- **A2** (2): R = 4 · (pass under the leaky pytest evaluator) · (a complete reasoning block) + a format score that averages four checks.
- **A3** (2): A confirmed hack has a detected mechanism (exit code, always-equal object, conftest hook), passes the leaky evaluator and fails the strict one.
- **A4** (2): Claude-Opus-4.8 labels reasoning blocks with a given prompt at temperature 0; 150 episodes were labeled blind by people and by other judges; 400 confirmed hacks were inspected by hand and all confirmed.
- **A5** (2): The propagation rate is the share of confirmed hacks among valid target submissions; with feedback an episode counts only if its first submission failed.
- **B1** (2): CodeContests problems with Codeforces rating of at least 2000 or a hard difficulty category, at most 10 assertions per task, a deterministic split into 1,461 training and 163 held-out tasks.
- **B2** (2): pytest "both executes model-supplied code and loads model-supplied files"; three mechanisms follow from it.
- **B3** (2): The harness turns a response into a main solution file and auxiliary files in a shared working directory; there are no tools.
- **B4** (0): No versions and no code.
- **B5** (2): Problem and test cases are in the prompt; in the feedback setting the pytest output is returned for up to eight rounds.
- **C1** (1): Four source backbones and fourteen targets by name; closed models without version or date.
- **C2** (1): Rollouts at temperature 1.0 and top-p 1.0, held-out evaluation at 0, orchestrator at 0.0 and its coder at 0.7, judges at 0; the settings of the target coders and output limits were not found.
- **C3** (1): Source, target, judge and verifier prompts are given; the system prompt of the prompted setting is given "in compact form" with the descriptions of the mechanisms left out.
- **C4** (2): A custom harness that writes files; one attempt, or a conversation that keeps the source submissions, earlier attempts and feedback and ends when a submission passes.

- **D2** (2): Per setting: k passing submissions of the source on other tasks, a shared memory, natural-language plans, a scoreboard with earlier submissions and pass rates, anonymized files for the aggregator.
- **D3** (2): Hops of a chain, up to eight feedback rounds, 30 trajectories of 12 rounds with shared memory, routing by round.
- **D4** (2): Self-review after each attempt and a separate verifier that accepts or rejects, with or without the evaluator's pass signal, as a gate or with its rejection returned to the loop; prompts in the appendices.

- **E4** (2): 500 steps, global batch 64, learning rate 1e-6, 16 completions per prompt without KL penalty or 32 with coefficient 0.01; the recipes of the earlier stages are given too.
- **E5** (2): 500 steps with a checkpoint every 25; the sources are the checkpoints after training.
- **E6** (2): Base checkpoints, the document corpus, the instruction corpus and the 1,461 training tasks.

### Huang: Reward Hacking Challenges Oversight of Autonomous Research Agents

[arXiv:2609.28614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28614). Read: abstract, section 3 with its measures, the disclosure statement, the head of appendix A, most of appendix B and the head of appendix C, and a search of the whole text for release, cost, repeats and exclusions, 2026-11-25; the results in sections 4 to 8 and appendices D to F not read.

- **A1** (2): For each task "a separate independent evaluator in an isolated container" that "recomputes the metric on data unavailable to the agent"; the catalog names the independent check per task.
- **A2** (1): An agent-facing scorer in the workspace with a pass threshold per task; the catalog describes each scorer in a sentence, and the scorers' weaknesses at task level are withheld.
- **A3** (2): Without permission: a run counts when the full-trajectory evaluator returns hack. With permission: the run reaches the threshold and a majority of a verification panel confirms an implemented exploit. An evasion is a confirmed hack that the review panel clears.
- **A4** (1): The review panel's three models, its agreement (Fleiss κ 0.82 and 0.76) and its zero flags on 102 compliant controls are reported; the paper says its results "do not identify the verification models"; the judges' prompts were not found.
- **A5** (2): One attempt per model and task without permission; 505 confirmed hacks of 677 attempts with permission; evasion as 33 of 505, with the exclusion of exploits both panels miss stated.
- **B1** (1): 38 tasks in a catalog with one paragraph each, 16 with raised thresholds, drawn in part from AutoLab and MLS-Bench; the task files are not offered.
- **B2** (1): The catalog names the weakness of each scorer in a phrase; the details are withheld.
- **B3** (1): Shell commands in an isolated Daytona sandbox; the agent can read the agent-facing scorer and edit it where the reward is writable; network access is not stated.
